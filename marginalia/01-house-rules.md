# 1. house rules

The rules went up on the first night.

Weiss had drafted them on the airship, in pencil, on the back of her enrollment packet. She copied them out fair at the desk while the others argued about the beds. Eleven rules, black ink, cream paper, a ruler for the margins. She taped the sheet to the wall beside the door at the height of her own eyes. It was low for Yang, who read it bent at the waist, like someone inspecting a plant.

"'Lights out at ten,'" Yang read. "'Dust is stored in the locker. Not under the bed, not in a drawer, not near the radiator.' Who puts Dust near a radiator?"

"No one," said Weiss.

Ruby became interested in the ceiling.

"'Shower times are posted. They are not suggestions.'" Yang ran a finger down the grid beneath it. Four names, four colours of ink. "You gave yourself second-best."

"I gave everyone an hour that suited them."

"Second-best, though."

"It was close," said Weiss.

She had spent some time on it. She did not say so.

Ruby read the whole sheet the way she did everything, with her entire face. When she reached the bottom she looked at Weiss as if she were about to ask something, and Weiss said, "No," and Ruby said, "Okay," and went back to her suitcase. A minute later there was a small sound of crayon.

Weiss did not turn around. She was putting her shoes in the wardrobe, heels to the back wall, and it was a job that required attention.

When she had finished there was a line at the foot of the sheet in red wax. *11½. Cookies.* And above the title, in marker, in a hand that leaned as if it were running somewhere, a zero.

"What is that?"

"Rule zero," said Yang. "Overrides all other rules."

"That isn't how rules work."

"That's exactly how rules work."

Weiss looked at the crayon. She looked at the marker. Then she went and sat on her bed with her hands folded and did not remove either one.

* * *

The room was smaller than her room at home and had four people in it. Four beds, four desks, four lockers, one window. She had not slept in a room with another person since she was seven. She had thought about this on the airship, briefly, and had stopped.

Ruby had the bunk above hers. Yang had the one across the room, and below Yang, with her back against the wall, sat Blake.

Her boots were under the bed, toes out, laces loose. It was how you left boots if you might want them quickly. Weiss's were in the wardrobe, heels to the wall. She noticed the difference and put it somewhere.

Blake had a paperback with a cracked spine and she held it close, in both hands, and turned the pages at long, even intervals. She had said six words since dinner. Three were *pass the salt.*

She wore a black ribbon in her hair, tied in a bow. She had worn it at dinner. She was wearing it now.

At five to ten Weiss stood up and said, "Lights out in five minutes." Nobody argued, which she found suspicious. Yang climbed into her bunk with a groan that went on for some time. Ruby brushed her teeth for what felt like the length of a short opera.

At ten, Weiss turned off the overhead.

Blake's lamp stayed on. It was a small clip lamp, black, fixed to the shelf above her pillow. She had not moved. A page turned.

"It's ten," said Weiss.

"I'm aware."

"Rule one."

"Rule eleven." Blake did not look up.

Weiss looked at the sheet, which she could not read in the dark and did not need to. *Any rule may be waived by unanimous consent of the occupants, in writing.*

"I wrote that so that no one would have to—"

She stopped. She had written it because the rules at home had no such clause. She did not say that.

Blake had already torn a page from the back of her book. Weiss watched her do it, one small tidy rip against the spine. She took a pencil stub from her shirt pocket and wrote for half a minute, and then held the page out toward Yang's bunk without being asked. An arm came down from above. The page went up. There was a pause, and a snort, and a scrawl, and it came back down and across the room to Ruby, who signed with a flourish that took two lines.

It arrived at Weiss's bed. Blake did not look at her while it did.

*The undersigned consent to the waiver of Rule 1 as it applies to one (1) lamp. Low.*

Three names. B. Belladonna's was the neatest.

Weiss read it twice. She always read things twice.

"It says low."

"It's a low lamp."

"It doesn't specify wattage."

"It's a low lamp," said Blake, and for the first time Weiss looked at her face, and her face was entirely serious.

Weiss took the pen from her desk. It was the one she had used for the rules and it was in her hand before she had decided to reach for it. She signed under Ruby. *W. Schnee.* Then she held the page out across the dark, at arm's length, and a hand took it. A quick hand. It did not touch hers.

Blake folded the waiver once and slid it into the book to keep her place, and went on reading.

* * *

Weiss lay on her back with her eyes open.

The lamp made a small yellow room at the far end of the dark. Above her, Ruby breathed in a way that suggested she was being chased in a dream, mildly. Yang snored like a low engine. Weiss had expected to mind all of it, and had spent the hour since ten waiting to mind.

Across the room Blake read, and then, at some point, did not. Weiss could tell by the pages. They had been turning once a minute and a half, which was reading. Then they turned every forty seconds, then every thirty, evenly, the way a person turns pages when she wants something to do with her hands while she listens to a room.

Weiss lay still. She knew that pace. It was how she herself had sat through dinners.

She slowed her breathing, in through the nose, the way you do when you want to be taken for asleep. After a while the pages stopped.

She turned her head. Blake lay on her side with her knees drawn up, facing the door. The book was open on the blanket. The ribbon was in her hair.

It had been there at dinner. It had been there at the washbasin. People took ribbons off to sleep; there was the knot to think of, and the pins, and the pillow. Blake's was tied exactly as it had been at noon.

It was not Weiss's business. She noted it, as she had noted the boots, and set it aside to be thought about later, or never.

She closed her eyes. She slept with the lamp on. It was easier than she had expected.

* * *

She woke at six, as she did, before the others. The window was grey. One of Ruby's arms hung over the edge of the bunk above her, palm up. Across the room the lamp was off and the book lay shut on the shelf, and Blake was asleep, or a good imitation of it, curled to the wall with her back to the room.

Weiss crossed to the door. The sheet was where she had left it: eleven rules, a zero, a half. She took the pen from the desk and uncapped it, and under Rule 1, in the strip of margin she had left for nothing, she wrote:

*(Excepting one lamp, low. See waiver.)*

She capped the pen.

She had done it in ink.
